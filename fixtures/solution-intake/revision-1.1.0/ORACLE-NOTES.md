## Readings needed to resolve ambiguities

1. **Item identity versus keep-scope evidence.** Correspondence uses exactly the §3.1 item content, not a decision's larger §3.2 scope. A node can have correspondence `same` while a decision about it requires re-review. For example, address edits leave n.line1-temp's nodeContent unchanged.

2. **Reason attribution for node decisions.** Section 4.1 explicitly defines evidence-changed by the item's §3.1 fingerprint. I apply that rule to each item in the node decision's keep scope: the node, its one-hop claims, owned edges, and anchored issues; virtual decisions additionally include the closure members and their one-hop contents. I do not replace a node's §3.1 fingerprint by its entire oneHop when assigning a decision reason. Consequently a changed claim or edge is named directly, and an unchanged nodeContent does not receive evidence-changed merely because its oneHop changed. This differs from some §7 examples. It also means nested changed items are not hidden inside an aggregate node reason. Dependencies of a claim decision are its transitive claim dependencies and the edges referenced by the target or any reached claim, with visited-set deduplication. A node decision does not acquire arbitrary transitive dependency content beyond the scope §3.2 specifies.

3. **Assessment and plan node evidence.** Sections 4.4 and 4.5 explicitly specify oneHop fingerprints for their node scope items. Those more specific instructions control for assessments and plans: a changed oneHop is reported as evidence-changed with that S1 node id, even when its correspondence is same. Assessments compare closure nodes by oneHop and closure edges by edgeContent; claims embedded in those oneHop values do not receive additional standalone assessment reasons. Plans compare only their own mappings[].nativeNodeId values by oneHop. Thus the same edit can produce a claim reason on a decision and a node reason on an assessment or plan.

4. **Target versus scope classification.** The target is assigned target-absent/target-ambiguous/target-unkeyed, rather than a duplicate scope-item reason for the very same item. Other items are classified even when the target is missing. Missing targets suppress all fingerprint/closure comparisons for that document, as §4.1 requires, but do not suppress global reasons or decision-reference checks. A node decision's one-hop children remain scope items, so a missing node can be accompanied by reasons for its missing claims and edges. An issue decision's anchor is a scope item under §3.2 and is classified independently.

5. **Parent ambiguity propagates to claims.** The claim procedure is described as identical to the edge procedure; this includes requiring the subject node to be matched. In pair 05, c.plc1-address and c.plc1-credential are therefore ambiguous because n.plc1 is ambiguous, although each claim base occurs only once. Virtual decision-3 includes both claims in the oneHop of its closure member n.plc1, so it gets all three scope-item-ambiguous reasons. An edge's target node being ambiguous does not by itself make that edge's owner/base correspondence ambiguous. Edge content can still contain the node key; closure membership checks separately expose n.plc1's ambiguity. Likewise a claim decision with no dependencies does not acquire its subject node as additional evidence scope.

6. **Both sides of closure scope.** A member that still exists in S2 but leaves the recomputed closure is not scope-item-absent. Absence means absent in the snapshot correspondence, not absent from the new closure traversal. Closure membership/edge/state/write differences receive closure-changed instead. S2 closure members are also checked for ambiguous identity; when their keys correspond to an S1 scope item, the reason uses its S1 id. No new S2-only ambiguity requiring an otherwise unavailable S1 ref occurs in these fixtures.

7. **Removing members from closureView.** Section 4.1 says to remove members but immediately includes membership in the differences to report. I remove the member evidence hashes, retain the sorted member keys for membership comparison, and compare all the other fields. Changed member oneHop evidence alone is evidence-changed, not closure-changed. This resolves the wording in favor of its explicit inclusion of membership; deleting members entirely would not alter these fixture outputs, because every changed membership here also changes other closureView fields. Edge content remains in closureView, so a changed closure edge can produce both evidence-changed and closure-changed.

8. **Capture is part of closureView.** captureScope is not removed for closure comparison. A capture-only edit therefore produces closure-changed for an assessment as well as capture-changed. A virtual node decision can receive closure-changed because of captureScope even though capture-changed itself is reserved for assessments/plans. A declared node decision does not acquire closure scope.

9. **Reasons accumulate across verdict precedence.** Orphaned and needs-mapping do not short-circuit reason collection. Globals, scope classifications, decision-not-carried, and assessment-not-reusable are included wherever independently applicable. Plans inherit the assessment's verdict category for precedence, not a copy of the assessment's reasons. In particular, a plan can be needs-mapping without its own scope-item-ambiguous reason, and orphaned without target-absent on the plan's assessment target.

10. **Supersession is target-based, not evidence-basis-based.** Under §4.2 the last decision with the same (target.type, target.id) is the head regardless of evidenceBasis, decision value, or whether the last decision can be carried. supersededBy points to that last head; superseded entries are emitted in S1 chain order (the specification gives no separate ordering for this array). Assessments/plans retain their actual S1 decisionRefs for the decision-not-carried check: a reference to a superseded decision is not silently redirected to its head. Only heads receive verdicts.

11. **Plan target representation.** The permitted schema identifies the plan verdict target type as `assessment`. The source is body.assessmentRef.id. No S2 assessment documents, assessment-copy id rule, or function output generating assessment documents are supplied. I use toId null for every plan, including reusable plans, rather than invent a new assessment id or reuse an S1 id as an S2 document id. `reusable` describes the stated ability to create a translated assessment, not the existence of such a document in this input.

12. **Candidates are restricted to absent node targets.** Candidate reporting follows §§2.3 and 4.7 literally. An absent node gets every unmatched S2 node with equal nodeContent, sorted by S2 id; no selection or inferred mapping follows. Only verdicts whose actual target is that absent node receive those candidates. Claim decisions do not inherit their subject node's candidates, and plan targets are assessments, so their candidate lists are empty. A candidate node may contain different attached claims/edges because candidate equality only tests nodeContent.

13. **Keys, optional fields, and serialization.** Identity tuples are the arrays in §2.1; the declaration's object-form node keys are normalized to those tuples for substitution. Base uniqueness and refinement are determined within each snapshot before substitution; node keys inside S1 fingerprints are then substituted. Missing state, stateKinds, access, rule, edge target, and candidates become null where specified. Missing outputs and dependency lists become empty lists. All fixture snapshots are 1.1.0. Candidate-node arrays use the schema's candidateNodeIds field. JCS is the json.dumps recipe supplied in the task. Lists designated sorted use their element JCS strings; final correspondence, verdict, reason, candidate, and carried arrays use the explicit output order rules. Reasons are deduplicated by (code, ref).

14. **Identity invariance and explicit ids.** The §1 and §7 invariance statements cannot mean byte-identical outputs after changing ids or a snapshot digest: correspondence/verdict target ids and §4.6 copy ids must expose those changes. I read these statements as invariance of matching, fingerprint comparisons, and verdict semantics under consistent renumbering, with the explicitly reported ids/pins translated as required. Profile change is the document digest comparison in §3.3, even if profile content could otherwise appear equivalent. Context comparison sorts the supplied context entries; capture comparison uses coverage.capture only.

15. **Limits of general wording.** If an edit only adds a new S2 one-hop claim/issue, §3.2 changes while §4.1 may offer no changed S1 §3.1 item to name. The rules do not provide a fully general reason attribution for that situation. I have not invented a new reason code or an S2 ref. No provided verdict relies on resolving that case: pair 06's new issue accompanies a changed existing edge, and closureView also changes. Similarly, rules for S2-only ambiguous scope items with no S1 ref do not determine a general ref; no such unmatched ambiguity occurs here. These observations do not change any emitted result.
